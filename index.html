Key Vocabulary

Formal Wear
1. Blazer (سترة رسمية): A type of jacket that looks like a suit jacket but is cut more casually.
  • He wore a navy blue blazer to the dinner.
  • Does this blazer match my trousers?
  • She prefers a wool blazer in the winter.
  • I need to get my blazer dry-cleaned.
2. Cuff (كُمّ / ردن): The end part of a sleeve, where the material is turned back.
  • He fastened the buttons on his shirt cuffs.
  • The cuffs of his jacket were slightly frayed.
  • Fold your cuffs before you start washing.
  • I like shirts with contrasting colors on the cuffs.
3. Tailored (مُفصّل): Specifically made to fit a particular person perfectly.
  • He looks great in that tailored suit.
  • Tailored clothes are usually more expensive.
  • She had her dress tailored for the wedding.
  • I prefer a tailored fit over a baggy one.
Casual Wear
4. Hoodie (سترة بقلنسوة): A sweatshirt with a hood attached to it.
  • I love wearing a warm hoodie on rainy days.
  • He pulled the hood of his hoodie over his head.
  • That oversized hoodie looks very comfortable.
  • Don't forget your hoodie; it's chilly outside.
5. Cardigan (سترة صوفية): A knitted sweater that opens at the front with buttons or a zip.
  • My grandmother knitted this wool cardigan.
  • It's easy to layer a cardigan over a t-shirt.
  • She wore a long, grey cardigan to the office.
  • Button up your cardigan if you are cold.
6. Outfit (ملابس / طقم): A set of clothes worn together.
  • That's a very stylish outfit you're wearing!
  • I need to choose an outfit for my job interview.
  • She bought a whole new outfit for the party.
  • This outfit doesn't really suit the occasion.

Common Expressions

1. To get dressed: The general process of putting clothes on.
  • Hurry up and get dressed!
  • It takes her an hour to get dressed.
  • I got dressed quickly this morning.
2. To fit like a glove: When a piece of clothing is the perfect size and shape.
  • I tried the dress on and it fits like a glove.
  • These new boots fit like a glove.
  • It’s hard to find jeans that fit like a glove.
3. To dress up: To wear formal or special clothes for an event.
  • We need to dress up for the gala tonight.
  • Kids love to dress up for Halloween.
  • You don't need to dress up; it's a casual party.
4. To suit someone: When a style or color looks good on a person.
  • That blue shirt really suits you!
  • Short hair doesn't suit me at all.
  • I don't think this hat suits my face.
5. To look for: When you are searching for a specific item in a shop.
  • I'm looking for a pair of black trousers.
  • What are you looking for in this section?
  • She is looking for a gift for her brother.
6. To be in fashion: When something is currently popular or trendy.
  • Bell-bottom jeans are back in fashion.
  • Is this style still in fashion?
  • He doesn't care about what is in fashion.
7. To try on: To put on a garment to see if it fits before buying.
  • Where can I try on these shirts?
  • Always try on shoes before you buy them.
  • I tried on five dresses but liked none of them.
8. To take off: The act of removing clothes.
  • Please take off your shoes at the door.
  • He took off his blazer because it was hot.
  • It's time to take off your wet clothes.

Real-Life Dialogues

1. In the Fitting Room

Assistant: How are those jeans fitting you?

Sarah: Actually, they fit like a glove! I'm impressed.

Assistant: That dark denim really suits your style.

Sarah: Thanks. I'm also looking for a matching blazer.

Assistant: We have some tailored options right over here.

Sarah: Great, I'll try on the navy one next.

2. Getting Ready for a Wedding

Mark: Are you almost ready? We need to leave soon.

John: Almost. I just need to fasten my cuffs.

Mark: Wow, you really dressed up for this!

John: Well, it is a formal wedding. How does my outfit look?

Mark: Very sharp. That tailored look is definitely in fashion.

John: Thanks! Let me just grab my coat and we can go.

Common Mistakes

1. I am wearing a cloth.

Correct: I am wearing clothes.

Why: "Cloth" refers to the fabric/material. "Clothes" is the plural noun for what we wear.

2. This shirt is not fitting me.

Correct: This shirt doesn't fit me.

Why: "Fit" is a stative verb; we usually use the simple present, not the continuous (-ing) form.

3. He wears a blue jean.

Correct: He is wearing blue jeans.

Why: "Jeans" is always plural because they have two legs. You can also say "a pair of jeans."

4. Dress up your jacket, it's cold.

Correct: Put on your jacket, it's cold.

Why: "Dress up" means to wear formal clothes. "Put on" is the action of dressing.

5. I like your costum.

Correct: I like your outfit.

Why: A "costume" is for actors or parties (like a clown). An "outfit" is regular clothing.

The Perfect Suit

Ahmed had an important interview. He wanted a tailored look, so he bought a navy blazer. When he tried it on, it fit like a glove. He checked the cuffs of his shirt to make sure they were clean. On his way, he saw a friend wearing a casual hoodie. "You look very sharp!" his friend said. Ahmed felt confident in his new outfit. He knew that dressing well was always in fashion for professional meetings.
